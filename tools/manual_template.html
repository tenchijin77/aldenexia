The world of Aldenexia

In the beginning, Aurethiel, Lady of the Dawn, and Durnhal the Forgefather kindled the First Flame, and from it the world was shaped. Ages of war followed: the Era of Shattered Crowns, the First Sundering that broke a continent, the Silver Tides, the Hollow Stars. Out of those wars came the Solstice Aegis, a covenant between light and shadow, bound into a relic that anchored the Veil between the living world and the Void.

Three hundred years ago the Moribund Order shattered it. The Aegis broke into five fragments and scattered across the world, and the Veil, with nothing holding it, began to tear. The first violet Veilfire burned within hours. The age since is called the Era of Veilfire.

The Luminar Covenant

The faith of the light. It recovered the Dawn Shard and has guarded it in Sanctum Pyraeth ever since. It has won battles for three centuries without winning the war, and has grown used to keeping secrets to keep people calm.

The Moribund Order

They are not simply villains. They believe the Aegis was a cage on the natural order and that death and the Void are the honest truth under the light. They hold two fragments and know where a third is kept.

It is now Year 300, Day 6 of Luminar. The Festival of First Light has just ended. In the southern villages, crops grow unnaturally large, wounds heal with geometric scars, and the dead visit their families in dreams. People call it a blessing. The Archmage Tenchijin Tenebralis, who touched the Dawn Shard, calls it the first stage of the Lightfall:

  1. The Blessing. Void energy looks like a gift: plenty, healing, peace. It is already here.
  2. The Awakening. The dead rise and greet their families. Prophets perform real miracles.
  3. The Revealed. Cities of writhing flesh. The sky torn open. Gods falling or fleeing.

These stages are not inevitable. They are directional. The direction can be changed. But the window for changing it is not unlimited, and it is narrowing.

From the Histories of Aldenexia, as recorded by the Arcane Synod

You begin outside Lumora, the sandstone city of the Solgrave Expanse, a desert of glowing oases and golden dunes. The Oasis Wardens keep its gate. The Sandveil merchants run its bazaar. Out on the dunes, the nomadic Djhanid keep to themselves and trade in water, not coin.

Your first hour

  1. Make a character

    Pick a race, then a class that race can be (see Races and Classes). Your race sets your starting stats, resistances and languages; your class sets your spells and role. A character has one name, letters only. A surname is earned in the world: at level 10, type /surname Name. New characters start outside Lumora's gate.

  2. Check your pack

    Press C. You start with a small bag holding a faded note, 20 iron rations, 20 water flasks and 5 torches, plus ragged clothes and a cape to put on. Warriors also get a rusty sword, Shadowblades and Woodstalkers a dagger, Aetherfists hand wraps, and spellcasters a dagger (Wildspeakers and Spiritweavers a staff). Drag each piece onto its equipment slot.

  3. Put your first spells on the bar

    Every class starts knowing two or three spells. Press K for the abilities book and drag them onto the action bar; 1–0 use them.

  4. Talk to the guards

    Walk up to a guard at the gate and press H to hail them. Words in their replies can be clicked to ask about them. Guard Reyna and Private Corwin have work for you (see Quests).

  5. Hunt near the gate

    Rats, sand vipers, bats and dune scarabs roam the dunes just outside. Press Tab to target the nearest, ` to attack, and right-click the corpse (or press G) to loot it. Turn on Auto-Loot on Kill in Esc → Options to have it happen by itself. Keep the rat tails: Kenji the cat wants them.

  6. Buy more spells

    Every other spell is sold on a scroll. Archivist Ilsabet copies the light and neutral classes' scrolls; Xalvyr Tenn sells the dark classes'. Right-click a scroll in your bags and choose Learn. Scrolls you already know are marked ✔ known.

  7. Try a trade

    Chop a fir tree, mine a tin vein or pick a thistle, then craft something. Every crafting skill has a few recipes you know from the start. Press L for your recipe book.

How the game works

Levels and experience

Characters go from level 1 to {{MAX_LEVEL}}. Killing monsters and finishing quests gives experience; the bar on the character sheet shows how far you are to the next level. Lumora Outskirts is for levels 1–5.

Health, mana and stamina

Health and mana come back on their own every few seconds, faster while sitting (X). Running uses stamina. Resting by a lit campfire speeds all three up.

Food and water

Satiety and thirst drain as you play. When either drops below 25 you eat or drink automatically from your bags. If you run out, Starving stops your health and stamina regeneration and Thirsty stops your mana regeneration, until you eat or drink.

Being knocked down, and dying

At 0 health you are downed and bleed out over 10 seconds. A heal from an ally in that time gets you back up. At −30 you die and wake at your bind point, the spot where you first entered the world, at full health. You keep your items and experience.

Poison and disease

Snakes and spiders sometimes poison you, and the undead sometimes infect you with disease: 5 damage every 6 seconds for a minute or more. They show on the status window with a yellow border. Cure spells remove them.

Falling

A drop of more than 6 metres hurts: 5% of your health for each metre beyond it.

Casting

Spells with a cast time fizzle if you move while casting; turning on the spot is fine. Melee abilities are instant. A spell has a range (most are 15 m) and a recast time before you can use it again.

Night, light and weather

Days turn to night. Carry a torch in your light slot (each burns a while), or rely on your race's dark sight. It rains now and then, and rain puts torches out. Any light gives away a hidden character.

Campfires

Rest by a campfire for 30 seconds and you get Warmth of the Campfire: faster health, mana and stamina regeneration for 15 minutes. You can light your own from a Firewood Bundle (Woodworking): right-click it in your bags and press Light. Your fire is a cooking station; its warmth reaches 15 m, and everyone inside that earns bonus experience from kills. You can't light one in town, in a fight, or within 20 m of another fire.

Groups

Type /invite name to group up (up to six, and they can be in another zone), /party to talk to the group and F1–F6 to target a member. Kills are shared: everyone in the group within 100 m of the kill gets experience, split evenly with a 10% bonus for each extra member, whether or not they struck a blow. A group holds together across zones: a member in another land shows on your group frame with the land's name, and still hears /party. Logging out leaves the group. Players walk through each other, so nobody can block a doorway. On the group window, a member beyond a heal's reach (16 m) or in another zone is faded. Group heals reach everyone nearby, and tank stances boost group members within 10 m.

Macros

Open the abilities book (K) on its Macros tab, or type /macro. A macro is up to five chat lines on one action-bar button, run top to bottom: /g Rooting %t, don't break it! then /cast Entangling Roots. %t is your target's name, %T adds its level and health, %s is you; /pause 2 waits two seconds. If a /cast fails, the macro stops there, so nobody is told about a spell that never happened. Each character has 24 macros, and the Shared page holds 24 more that every character on your account can use.

Your look

When you make a character, press Appearance… to set height, weight and muscle (and bust, waist and hips for women), and skin and hair colour, on a turning preview. Your look is kept for good; afterwards only your hair can change. Characters made before this can visit the Silvered Mirror (at the barber's, just inside the city's south gate) once to choose their look; after that the mirror changes your hair colour. Hair and beard styles and jewellery will come with new models.

Faction standing

Every people and power in Aldenexia keeps a standing with you, from −100 to +100. Your race sets where you start (the Covenant trusts humans, elves and dwarves; the Moribund Order favours trolls, ogres, lizardkin and dark elves). What you do moves it: killing a people's own lowers it ("Your faction standing with the Djhanid Clans got worse."), killing their enemies raises it, and a change spreads to their friends and rivals. Fall far enough and that people's warriors attack on sight; rise high enough and they count you an ally. The target window shows (Enemy) or (Ally).

Monsters that cast

Named monsters, and the tougher creatures farther out, have tricks of their own: webs that hold you, curses, poisons, spells that hit everyone near them, calls for help, and a frenzy near death. When one begins a spell you'll read "…begins to cast…" and see (casting …) on the target window: a stun or bash breaks it. A silence stops monsters casting, and stops you too; skills still work.

The law. In town (Lumora, or inside the Outskirts' walls), striking another player in PvP where a guard can see is a crime: a bounty in Lumora and lost standing with the Wardens (a duel isn't a crime). A guard who spots you will demand the fine: pay it, or resist, and every guard attacks you on sight. If the guards kill you, the fine comes out of your purse and whatever you couldn't pay stays on the books. Settle a bounty, or make amends for lost standing, with Magistrate Corvane at the Lumora courthouse.

Stealth. While hidden (the Shadowblade's Stealth, Shadowstep, invisibility) monsters don't notice you by sight, you show as a faint shape with [stealth] on your name, and attacking reveals you. A monster already fighting you keeps after you.

Fear. Some undead can terrify you. If the spell gets past your resistance you roll to keep your nerve: make it and you're only Shaken (weaker, clumsier, and you miss more); fail and you're Terrified: you run blindly, unable to fight or cast, until it wears off or someone cures it. Higher level and Wisdom help; so does a Lizardkin's cold blood.

Trading and the bank

Right-click another player (or type /trade name) to swap items and coin; both of you must accept. Oswin Coinwright, Lumora's banker, keeps what you can't carry. Coin runs copper, silver, gold.

Appraising

Target a monster and press I to size it up before a fight. Each appraisal trains your Perception, which makes later appraisals better.

Armour

Anyone can wear cloth. Heavier armour depends on your class: casters wear cloth only; Woodstalkers, Wildspeakers and Aetherfists leather; Shadowblades and Spiritweavers chain; the tanks, Troubadours and Lightmenders plate. Shields are for the tanks, Lightmenders, Troubadours, Spiritweavers and Wildspeakers. Each class's panel under Classes lists what it can wear, and an item's tooltip says whether you can.

Your bind point and gating home

You come back to your bind point when you die, with every spell on you gone (good and bad) and your pet sent away: summon it again, and its gear is still on it. At first it is where you entered the world. From level 4, casters (Lightmender, Spiritweaver, Wildspeaker, Gravecaller, Runecaster, Arcanist, Chaosborn) learn Attune Spirit: it binds the friendly player they target within 20 m, or themselves with no target, and they can cast it as often as they like (bind the whole group before a hard dungeon, one at a time). Everyone else asks a caster, or pays Soulbinder Maren at Lumora's Dawnspire Altar a silver. Each person's bind point can change once an hour. From level 6 every class has a gate spell (Recall to Sanctuary, Call of Nature, March Home, Death's Gate...) that takes you to your bind point. Neither works in the middle of a fight.

Duels and PvP

Players are allies until they choose otherwise. /duel name (or /duel with a player targeted) challenges them; if they accept, a three-second count, then you fight until one of you falls to 1 health (nobody dies), someone types /yield, you move more than 40 m apart, or five minutes pass. /pvp name declares a real fight: no consent needed, but they're warned and get five seconds, and only between level 5 and up, within 5 levels of each other. Then it's to the death, with the usual death rules, until ten minutes pass without a blow. Only the two of you are foes; to everyone else you're still allies.

Maps (Cartography)

You start with no map, just like in life. Cartographer Ivo Quill, by Lumora's south gate, sells the Scroll of Cartography (5 silver), Blank Parchment and a Charcoal Stick. Learn the scroll, carry the parchment and charcoal, and the land around you is drawn onto your map as you walk; press M to look at it. The map is a window you can move and resize; the mouse wheel zooms and dragging moves it. Right-click it to write a note; right-click a note to rub it out. Charting new ground raises the skill (to 100, fastest for Woodstalkers): place names and zone borders appear at 21, camps, people, resources and contour lines at 51, and patrol routes, rare creatures and hidden places at 76. Carry a compass and the map shows where you are and which way you face.

Perception

Keep your eyes open. Near interesting places (the gate, the docks, the mausoleum, the camps) your character may notice things others walk past: a detail appears in the chat. Each check is a d20 plus your Wisdom modifier plus a tenth of your Perception skill. Harder details need a sharper eye, and every check trains Perception, so the more you look, the more you see.

Logging out

Type /camp and wait 15 seconds without taking damage. Your character is saved where you stand.

Languages

Everyone in Aldenexia speaks something, and not everyone speaks Common. You start with your race's languages. What others say in a language you don't know arrives scrambled, and the less you know, the more it is scrambled. You learn a language by hearing it spoken and by speaking it where someone who knows it can hear. Choose the language you speak from the dropdown beside the chat box, or with /language name. A language you don't know at all can be started from a primer scroll. Your languages and their skill are listed at the end of the abilities book (K), under General Skills.

Races

Your race decides your starting statistics, your resistances to the ten kinds of damage, the languages you speak and whether you see in the dark. Some races are shunned in some places; the Vol'kyne are openly distrusted in Lumora. The classes each race can take are listed under Classes.

Racial traits are part of every character, old and new, and are worked out each time you log in. Skill bonuses count when you use the skill (a Dwarf smiths as if 15 points better) but don't count toward skill-ups or a recipe's minimum. A few traits wait on systems that don't exist yet (swamps, intimidation, research); they are marked planned.

Classes

Fourteen classes in four roles. Tanks hold a monster's attention and take the hits. Healers keep the group alive. Melee and caster damage classes kill things, up close or from a distance. Every class can solo in Lumora Outskirts. Open a class to see its full spell list, levels 1 to {{MAX_LEVEL}}.

Spells and abilities

"Spell" covers everything on your action bar: a Lightmender's heal, a Blademaster's Power Strike, a Troubadour's song. Each class's list is in its panel under Classes.

Caster travel

Three classes can carry their group across the land, but only to places they have been. Each has its own places of power, scattered across different lands, and none can use another's. Arcanist Spires were raised by the scholars of the Lycaeum before the Lightfall, fixed as precisely as coordinates. Wildspeaker Ruins are older than any city: places where the Green still remembers the world before the Lightfall. Chaosborn Rifts are wounds the Lightfall tore in the world, and they never closed. Find one of your own and right-click it to anchor it in memory: an Arcanist traces runes, a Wildspeaker sows a spectral seed, a Chaosborn presses their blood into it. When you cast, you choose which attuned site to go to. There are none near Lumora, and they are rarely on the main roads: you have to go looking.

Arcanist: Spectral Bridge

A 15-second channel. A shimmering bridge opens behind the Arcanist, who stays rooted as its anchor. The group runs across it; when the spell ends, the portal swallows the Arcanist too. Spatial Redoubt (level 10) folds the zone's entrance to you: slow, and completely safe.

Wildspeaker: Root-Tunnel

An 8-second channel. Spirit-vines erupt and drag everyone in the group within 8 m through the Green ley-lines; you all arrive with Nature's Ward, faster healing for two minutes. Wormhole (level 10) drags the group through the earth to the town gate: fast, and filthy.

Chaosborn: Chaos Rift

A flickering 5 to 8 second ritual opens a jagged hole in reality behind the Chaosborn. The group steps through; the Chaosborn follows when it ends. One arrival in twenty brings a Wild Surge. Phase Snap (level 10) is the panic button: 1.5 seconds, then a d20 Chaos Tax — lost mana, Aether-Sickness, a bent spoon in everyone's pack, or a lucky clean escape.

Learning spells

You start with your class's first two or three spells. Every other spell comes on a scroll: buy it, right-click it in your bags and choose Learn. You can learn a scroll before you reach its level, but you can't cast the spell until you do. The scroll's tooltip shows the level your class needs, in green or red.

Where to buy scrolls

Archivist Ilsabet Thornmere copies the scrolls of the light and neutral classes. She won't touch the dark classes' texts. For those, find Xalvyr Tenn at the Sandveil Bazaar: Voidknight, Gravecaller, Shadowblade and Chaosborn scrolls, and some shared ones like Taunt.

Upgrades

Some spells have Improved, Greater or Master versions. An upgrade needs its level to learn, then replaces the original in your book and on your action bar. Its icon is the original's, tinted gold (Improved, Greater) or violet (Master).

Passives

Spells marked passive are always on once learned, such as Improved Block or Weapon Mastery. There's nothing to cast.

Stances

Tanks and Aetherfists switch between stances on the stance bar: a defensive one to hold monsters, an offensive one to kill faster, a balanced one in between. The tanks' stances also help group members within 10 m. Shadowblades use the stance bar to hide.

Songs

Every Troubadour spell is a song. Clicking a song starts it playing, and it repeats by itself until you click it again. Songs keep playing while you move, and stop when you die.

Pets

Gravecallers, Voidknights, Woodstalkers, Wildspeakers, Spiritweavers and Lightmenders can summon pets. A pet follows you, fights your target, has its own frame, and comes back after you log out and in (but not after you die: summon it again). P opens its gear. A pet wears the weapon and shield you give it, for everyone to see. On Assist it joins what you fight; if you have a /focus set on your tank, it fights what the tank is fighting instead (a healer's pet can help without the healer swinging). Once it starts on something it stays on it until it dies (a wounded monster running away doesn't get away), unless you send it at a new target with its Attack button; then it moves on to your target by itself.

Pickpocketing

Shadowblades, Troubadours and Woodstalkers can pick a monster's pocket. From behind it always works, from the front about 40% of the time, while hidden about 70%. Fail and the monster notices you. Each monster can only be picked once.

Skills

Skills grow by use: swing a sword to raise Slashing Weapons, get hit to raise Defense, cast Evocation spells to raise Evocation. A skill can rise by at most {{SKILL_CAP}} points per character level, so at level 5 a skill tops out at {{SKILL_CAP_5}}. Your skills are on the character sheet and in the abilities book.

Crafting and gathering

Anyone can learn every trade. Gather raw materials from the land, refine them, and make gear, food, potions, tools and ammunition. Crafting in Lumora Outskirts is the first step. Later lands will extend every trade.

Gathering

Right-click a plant, ore vein or tree to gather from it. Forage needs nothing; Prospecting needs a Miner's Pick and Woodworking harvesting a Woodcutter's Axe, anywhere in your bags. Moving or being attacked interrupts you. A failed attempt finds nothing but doesn't use up the node.

Recipes

Each trade has a few recipes you know from the start. The rest come from recipe scrolls sold by the town's vendors: right-click and Learn. Press L for your recipe book, which shows what you can make, where, and what it needs. Drag the ingredients into the crafting window's slots: if they could make several things, a Make: picker lets you choose, and How many sets the batch. To use part of a stack, right-click it in your bags and choose Split.

Where to craft

Portable kits (sold by vendors) let you craft most recipes anywhere; right-click the kit and choose Open. A kit in a character-sheet slot also works as an 8-slot bag for that trade's materials. Town stations, on Crafters' Row in the city of Lumora (through the gate passage, then west along the wall), take every recipe of their trade. Blacksmithing is forge-only. Cooking also works at any campfire.

Success and skill-ups

Every recipe has a base success chance, plus 1% for each skill point above its minimum, up to 98%. A failure uses up the ingredients. A critical success makes extra. Each attempt may raise your skill until the recipe turns trivial, 15 points above its minimum; after that it still works but teaches nothing.

Tools and upgrades

Vendors sell a basic pick and axe. Tinkering makes better ones (Copper Pickaxe, Bronze Woodcutter's Axe): faster gathering and more critical finds. Tools never break.

Ammunition and light

A quiver in the ammo slot adds damage to every ranged attack and is never used up; bows need one to fire. Woodworking makes torches, and Tinkering makes a Copper Lantern that never burns out and shrugs off rain.

Armour sets

Each armour type has a nine-piece set a crafter can make: Oasis Linen (cloth, Tailoring), Dunehide (leather, Leatherworking), Copperlink (chain, Blacksmithing, from copper links) and Bronzeguard (plate, Blacksmithing). Two pieces of each are known from the start, the rest are patterns sold by Aldric and Borgrim, and each set's chest piece is a quest reward. Wear all nine pieces of one set for a bonus; an item's tooltip shows how many you have on.

Deconstruct

Put old gear in the slots of a kit or station and press Deconstruct to take it apart. Anything made by a recipe gives back half its ingredients; loot and starting clothes give basic materials (cloth: linen and thread; leather: scraps; metal: scrap metal). Use the trade that works the material — cloth at a tailoring kit, metal at the forge. It trains no skill.

Bronze is an alloy: smelt tin and copper, then combine 3 Copper Ingots and 1 Tin Ingot at the forge for 2 Bronze Ingots. There is no bronze ore.

Lumora Outskirts

The sun-baked land around Lumora's walls, for levels 1 to 5. The dunes by the gate are gentle: rats, vipers, bats and scarabs. Farther out are bandit camps, goblin camps, an old mausoleum with its graveyard and crypts, a spider den gone strangely wrong, and the deep dunes where the Hollowed wander. Sandcrest Landing, the docks, is the gateway to lands beyond.

Follow the roads. The signpost outside the gate reads: North — Lumora. West — Sandcrest Landing and the docks. East — the goblin camps.

The city. Lumora itself lies through the far north end of the gated town; walk on past the last buildings and you cross into the city. Inside: Crafters' Row with every crafting station, the Treasury Office (the bank), the Archive, Lira's stall by the Dawnspire Altar, the Sandveil Bazaar and the barber's mirror. Aldric stays by the Outskirts gate. The rest of the city is still being built.

Beyond the Outskirts. Far to the south, a gap in the land leads on to the Dustwind Plateaus (levels 4 to 9, and worse in the burial mounds): feral hounds, scorched sandstingers, Dustwalker raiders, the Djhanid, and older dead things. South again lie the Ashfall Dunes (levels 7 to 12): Bonegnawer gnoll camps, crystal sandshifters, the Mass Gravesite, Tak'Majir spies from the drow city of Xul-Dura, the Bleached Forest, and the Djhanid Encampment. Its worst, the phantom lord, the Tak'Majir spymaster, the Dustwalker commander and the rare sandshifter alpha, are levels 13 to 16 and need a group. Walk off the edge of a land and you cross into the next. If you fall there, you wake wherever you are bound.

The Djhanid are desert nomads, wary of strangers because Lumorans mistake them for the Dustwalker bandits. They never start a fight. Kill their people and they'll soon attack you on sight; kill Dustwalkers and Tak'Majir and they warm to you, and in time count you a friend.

Quests

Quests go into your journal (J) when you get them. Hail people (H): someone with work for you says so, and a highlighted word in their reply starts the quest when you click it. Picking up a quest's item starts it too. To hand something in, drag it from your bags onto the person; some quests want several different things, and you can bring them a few at a time. The journal shows each item a quest needs with its icon and how many you're carrying, and ticks it off once it's handed in.

{{QUESTS}}

Gate raids

Every 25 to 45 minutes, while players are near the gate, a band of bandits or goblins marches on it. They announce themselves. The guards go out to meet them and wear them down, but leave the killing blows to you. More raiders come when more players are around.

Bestiary

Most creatures attack when you come near. Scarabs keep to themselves unless you walk right up to them. named foes are tougher, drop better loot and take a while to return once killed; fight them with friends. A killed creature returns to its haunt a minute or more after it dies (never right next to you), so you can hold a spot and rest between fights.
